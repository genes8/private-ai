Strategic Decision Frame

Use this before asking AI or yourself to implement a non-trivial change. The goal is to make the human judgment explicit: why this change, why now, what tradeoffs, what evidence, and how it stays reversible.

1. Outcome
  • What user, operator, or developer pain changes?
  • What will be observably better after this ships?
  • What is deliberately not being solved?
2. Constraints
  • What interfaces, data, latency, security, cost, or rollout constraints matter?
  • Which constraints are facts, and which are assumptions?
  • What existing code shape should be respected?
3. Options
  • List at least two plausible approaches.
  • Name the boring option and the tempting option.
  • Explain why rejected options lose.
4. Verification
  • What test proves the main behavior?
  • What regression would be expensive to miss?
  • What manual or operational check closes the loop?
5. Change Shape
  • Can it be split into small, reviewable changes?
  • What refactor should be separate from behavior?
  • What can be merged safely before the full feature?
6. Reversibility
  • Can this be rolled back without data loss?
  • Does it need a feature flag, migration guard, or fallback path?
  • What would make the decision worth revisiting?

Copyable Mini-ADR

Use this for planning notes, PR descriptions, issue comments, or a real ADR when the decision is durable.

# Decision: {short active-voice title}

## Context
{What problem are we solving? What constraints and facts shape the choice?}

## Options
1. {Option A}: {main benefit, main cost}
2. {Option B}: {main benefit, main cost}
3. {Do nothing}: {cost of delay or reason this is acceptable}

## Decision
We will {chosen approach}.

## Consequences
- Positive: {what becomes easier, safer, faster, or clearer}
- Negative: {new coupling, cost, risk, or maintenance burden}
- Neutral: {what changes but is not clearly good or bad}

## Verification
- Automated: {unit/integration/contract/e2e checks}
- Manual: {review, screenshot, smoke, rollout check}
- Revisit when: {trigger that would make this decision stale}
Rule of thumb: strategic programming is not "think longer before coding." It is making the important judgment visible early enough that implementation work can be split, reviewed, tested, and reversed.
